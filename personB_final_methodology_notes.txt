PERSON B — FINAL METHODOLOGY AND RESULTS NOTES
ML MINI-PROJECT: PANCREATIC CANCER PROGNOSIS

1. PERSON B OBJECTIVE
Person B handled the survival-analysis component of the pancreatic cancer
prognosis project.

The objective was to characterize patient survival using Kaplan-Meier
analysis and determine whether CT-derived radiomic features provide
additional survival-related information beyond clinical variables using
Cox proportional hazards modelling.

2. SURVIVAL ANALYSIS CONCEPTS

Survival time:
The observed time from the beginning of follow-up until the event occurs
or the observation is censored. In this project the variable is
survival_time_days.

Event:
The outcome of interest is death.
- event = 1: death occurred.
- event = 0: observation was censored.

Censoring:
Censoring occurs when the event is not observed during the available
follow-up period. It means the later outcome is unknown.

Hazard:
The instantaneous risk or rate of experiencing the event at a particular
time, conditional on having survived up to that time.

Survival function:
S(t) represents the probability that a patient survives beyond time t.

3. DATASET

The clinical survival dataset contained:
- 138 patients
- 110 events
- 28 censored observations

The CT/radiomics cohort contained:
- 46 patients
- 41 events

The radiomics cohort is smaller because only patients with suitable
matched CT imaging and tumor segmentation were included.

4. KAPLAN-MEIER METHODOLOGY

Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to estimate survival probability over
time while accounting for censored observations.

Pipeline:

Patient data
     |
     v
Survival time + Event
     |
     v
Kaplan-Meier estimator
     |
     v
Survival probability
     |
     v
Survival curve

Two main analyses were performed:

A. Overall survival
A Kaplan-Meier curve was generated for the full 138-patient clinical
survival cohort.

B. 365-day risk-group survival
The predefined classification groups were compared:
- Class 0: known survival >= 365 days
- Class 1: death before 365 days

Patients censored before 365 days were excluded from the 365-day
classification cohort.

5. KAPLAN-MEIER RESULTS

Overall survival:
- 138 patients
- 110 events
- 28 censored
- Observed median follow-up: 442 days
- Kaplan-Meier estimated median survival: 603 days

Risk-group survival:
- Class 0 median survival: 815 days
- Class 1 median survival: 211 days
- Log-rank statistic: 188.4982
- Log-rank p-value: approximately 6.76e-43

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION:
The 365-day groups were defined directly from survival outcome information.
Therefore, their strong Kaplan-Meier separation is expected and should not
be presented as independent predictive validation.

6. COX PROPORTIONAL HAZARDS MODEL

The Cox proportional hazards model was used to examine associations
between predictor variables and the hazard of death.

General pipeline:

Predictor features
        |
        v
Cox Proportional Hazards model
        |
        +--> Coefficient
        |
        +--> Hazard Ratio
        |
        +--> Confidence Interval
        |
        +--> p-value
        |
        +--> C-index

Three models were developed:
- Clinical Cox
- Radiomic Cox
- Combined Clinical + Radiomic Cox

7. CLINICAL COX MODEL

Clinical predictors:
- Age

- Tumor Grade

The clinical Cox model was evaluated on the 46-patient radiomics cohort
so that it could be compared fairly with the radiomic and combined models.

Clinical Cox C-index:
0.6606

Result:

The purpose of correlation filtering was to reduce redundant predictors,

Each of the 61 remaining radiomic features was screened using a
univariate Cox proportional hazards model.

The features were ranked using their statistical significance and a
small final feature set was selected because the radiomics cohort
contains only 46 patients.

Three radiomic features were selected:

1. original_gldm_LargeDependenceHighGrayLevelEmphasis
2. original_glcm_MCC
3. original_glszm_LargeAreaEmphasis

13. C-INDEX

The concordance index measures how well a survival model ranks patients
according to relative risk.

Approximate interpretation:
- 0.5 -> random concordance
- 1.0 -> perfect concordance

Final results:

Clinical Cox:
0.6606

Radiomic Cox:
0.6091

Combined Cox:
0.7081

The combined model had the highest apparent C-index.

14. HAZARD RATIO

For a Cox coefficient beta:

HR = exp(beta)

Interpretation:
- HR = 1 -> no approximate change in hazard.
- HR > 1 -> higher hazard.
- HR < 1 -> lower hazard.

The analysis produced hazard ratios, Cox coefficients, 95% confidence
intervals and p-values for the evaluated Cox models.

15. PROPORTIONAL HAZARDS ASSUMPTION

The Cox model assumes that the relative hazards associated with predictors
remain approximately proportional over time.

The proportional-hazards assumption was tested for:
- Clinical Cox
- Radiomic Cox
- Combined Cox

All tested variables had p-values greater than 0.05.

Conclusion:
No statistically significant violation of the proportional hazards
assumption was detected in the evaluated models.

16. MODEL COMPARISON

Model                         C-index
Clinical Cox                  0.6606
Radiomic Cox                  0.6091
Combined Cox                  0.7081

Interpretation:
The combined clinical-radiomic model had the highest apparent concordance
in this exploratory 46-patient cohort.

17. LIMITATIONS

- The radiomics cohort contains only 46 patients.
- There are 107 initial radiomic features for only 46 patients.
- This creates substantial overfitting risk.
- Feature selection and model fitting were performed on the available
  cohort.
- The reported C-indices are apparent exploratory estimates rather than
  independently validated performance.
- Independent external validation would be required to establish
  generalizability.
- The Kaplan-Meier risk groups were defined using survival outcome
  information, so their separation should not be treated as independent
  predictive validation.

18. FINAL PERSON B CONCLUSION

Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to characterize overall survival and the
survival patterns of the predefined 365-day risk groups.

For the radiomic survival analysis, 107 initial radiomic features were
reduced to 61 using correlation filtering, followed by univariate Cox
screening that selected three radiomic features.

Three Cox models were compared on the 46-patient radiomics cohort.
The clinical-only, radiomic-only and combined models achieved apparent
C-indices of 0.6606, 0.6091 and 0.7081 respectively.

The combined clinical-radiomic model therefore showed the highest
apparent concordance in this exploratory analysis.
